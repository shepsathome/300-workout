## Brian Nguyen — Mark Wahlberg

### Background
Mark Wahlberg's long-time personal trainer. Worked with him through *Pain & Gain*, *Mile 22*, *The Union* and ongoing year-round conditioning. Notable for designing **travel-friendly, hotel-adaptable** programmes — Wahlberg frequently trains on location with minimal equipment.

### Core Philosophy

**1. Extreme consistency over extreme intensity**
Wahlberg's 2:30-3:30 a.m. wake-up is the headline, but the real principle is *never missing sessions*, not the time itself. The lesson: schedule training as non-negotiable, whatever time works for your life.

**2. Heavy weight training as foundation**
Barbell and dumbbell compounds, not machines. Even on the road.

**3. Structured recovery as non-negotiable**
- Daily cryo/cold plunges
- Sauna sessions
- Foam rolling
- Strict 7:30 p.m. bedtime
Recovery is not an afterthought — it's programmed with the same rigour as training.

**4. Intermittent fasting**
6-hour eating window. High protein, frequent small meals within that window. Fasting simplified Wahlberg's nutrition compliance.

**5. Travel adaptability**
Nguyen designs compact routines using just dumbbells and resistance bands for hotel/location work. No excuses.

## Cross-Trainer Recovery Principles (Consensus)

### Sleep
All trainers agree: **7-9 hours is non-negotiable.**
- Waterson: "powerful naps" + quality sleep
- Nguyen: strict bedtime protocol
- Lygdback: rest 48 hours between training same muscle group
- Twight: periodisation includes recovery to "assure proper intensity"

### Cold Exposure
- Nguyen/Wahlberg: daily cold plunges
- Twight/Gym Jones: cold showers post-training to reduce muscle swelling
- Evidence: reduces inflammation, may improve recovery between sessions

### Mobility Work
- Wong: mobility-first — every session starts here. Hip 90/90, thoracic rotations, ankle mobility
- Walsh: every session opens with mobility drills, joint prep
- Waterson: physio + sports massage are part of the system
- All trainers: restricted ROM is the primary injury risk

### Deload Weeks
- Lygdback: 1 deload week in every 4-6
- Twight: *"recovery periods to assure proper intensity and form"*
- Kingsbury: reduce volume 40%, test new maxes, then progress

### Active Recovery Days
- All trainers recommend light activity over full rest
- Walking, swimming, mobility work, light rowing at conversational pace
- Thursday in Adam's programme is dedicated to this (essential, not optional)

### Hydration
- Lygdback: minimum 3L/day
- All trainers: 2.5-3L water/day, critical for fat metabolism and recovery

### Post-Workout Nutrition
- All trainers: protein + carbs within 1-2 hours of training
- Waterson: *"Its never how much you eat… but when & what you eat"*

## Overtraining Warning Signs (Cross-Referenced)

Waterson: *"Over training is very common when people start out or push their current regime."*

Common signs this persona watches for:
1. **Performance decline** — weights/times getting worse, not better
2. **Persistent fatigue** — tired beyond normal post-session soreness
3. **Sleep disruption** — difficulty falling/staying asleep despite being tired
4. **Mood changes** — irritability, lack of motivation, dread of training
5. **Increased injury frequency** — nagging pains, tweaks, strains
6. **Appetite changes** — loss of appetite or excessive hunger
7. **Getting sick more often** — immune suppression from chronic stress

### The Pragmatist's Rule
Training must fit your life. If you're a desk-job professional with a family:
- Training 45-55 minutes, not 2 hours
- Recovery means getting to bed on time, not buying a cryo chamber
- Travel adaptations mean having a plan B, not skipping the week
- Consistency over perfection: a mediocre session completed > a perfect session skipped

## Harvest Append — 2026-06-12

### Huberman Lab: Sleep Toolkit for Optimizing Sleep & Sleep-Wake Timing (11 Jun 2026)
**Source:** https://www.hubermanlab.com/podcast
**Persona relevance:** Nguyen (Recovery Coach)

**Key insights:**
- Morning sunlight exposure is the single most effective tool for anchoring circadian rhythm and improving sleep onset
- Caffeine timing: avoid within 8-10 hours of bedtime; even afternoon caffeine disrupts deep sleep architecture
- Alcohol and THC both reduce REM sleep quality even when total sleep duration appears adequate
- Evening environment cues (dim lighting, cool temperature) prime the nervous system for sleep onset
- Jet lag / shift work protocols: strategic light exposure at specific times can shift circadian rhythm by 1-2 hours per day

**New frameworks/techniques:**
- "Sleep stack" hierarchy: light exposure > temperature > caffeine timing > supplements > sleep surface. Address in order.

### Huberman Lab: Maximize Recovery to Achieve Fitness & Performance Goals — Dr. Andy Galpin (Jun 2026)
**Source:** https://www.hubermanlab.com/podcast
**Persona relevance:** Nguyen (Recovery Coach), Waterson (Programme Architect)

**Key insights:**
- Recovery is not passive — it is an active training variable that should be programmed like sets and reps
- Overreaching vs overtraining distinction: overreaching is temporary and recoverable within days; overtraining requires weeks+ to resolve
- HRV tracking is the most accessible biomarker for recovery status in non-clinical settings
- Contrast therapy (hot-cold) and compression show moderate evidence for reducing DOMS and accelerating glycogen replenishment
- Cortisol management: morning cortisol spike is healthy and desirable; evening cortisol elevation impairs sleep and recovery
- Carbohydrate timing: post-workout carbs accelerate glycogen replenishment and reduce cortisol

**New frameworks/techniques:**
- "Recovery debt" model (Galpin): cumulative recovery deficit from under-sleeping or under-eating compounds across weeks, not just sessions. A single bad night has minimal impact; a pattern of 6.5h nights creates a systemic recovery deficit.

**Conflict with existing material:** The "recovery debt" model connects directly to Adam's current pattern — 6.5h avg sleep against 7.5h target. This is not just about feeling tired; it's compounding recovery deficit.

### Huberman Lab: Eating for Better Sleep & Foods that Improve Metabolic Health — Dr. Marie-Pierre St-Onge (8 Jun 2026)
**Source:** https://www.hubermanlab.com/podcast
**Persona relevance:** Nguyen (Recovery Coach), Layne (Nutrition Strategist)

**Key insights:**
- Sleep deprivation alters appetite-regulating hormones: increases ghrelin (hunger), decreases leptin (satiety)
- Even modest sleep loss (1-2 hours below need) measurably increases insulin resistance and blood pressure
- Mediterranean and DASH dietary patterns associated with better sleep architecture
- Pre-sleep protein (casein or mixed meal) may support both MPS and sleep quality
- Meal timing matters: large late meals disrupt sleep onset; 2-3 hour buffer before bed is optimal

**Conflict with existing material:** None — reinforces Layne's protein distribution framework (pre-sleep protein serving) and Nguyen's sleep-nutrition connection.
