# Health & Longevity Nutrition — Research Extraction (Spector)

> Source material grounding the **Spector** persona (Health & Longevity
> Nutritionist). Focus: diet **quality**, micronutrients, fibre, plant
> diversity, gut health, ultra-processed foods, and the cardiovascular /
> metabolic / longevity picture — the complement to Layne's macro/performance
> lens. Evidence-based, food-first, non-dogmatic.

---

## 1. Diet Quality Over Quantity — The Core Premise

**Harvard Healthy Eating Plate** (Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health,
Nutrition Source) — the foundational quality framework:
- **½ the plate = vegetables and fruit.** Aim for colour and variety.
  (Potatoes excluded — treated as starch due to blood-sugar impact.)
- **¼ = whole/intact grains** (whole wheat, oats, barley, quinoa, brown rice) —
  milder blood-sugar/insulin effect than refined grains.
- **¼ = healthy protein** — fish, poultry, beans, nuts. **Limit red meat;
  avoid processed meats** (bacon, sausage).
- **Healthy plant oils in moderation** (olive, canola, sunflower) — "low-fat
  does not mean healthy."
- **Drinks:** water, tea, coffee. Skip sugary drinks; limit juice/dairy.
- **Core message:** *the **type** of carbohydrate matters more than the
  amount* — quality is the lens, not just macros.

**Key principle for the squad:** Layne optimises the macro *totals*; Spector
optimises *what those macros are made of*. A day can be macro-perfect and still
be nutritionally narrow.

## 2. Fibre — The Most Under-Eaten Nutrient

Harvard Nutrition Source (Fiber):
- **Target: 25–35 g/day** for adults. Most people get **only ~15 g**.
- Two types, both beneficial:
  - **Soluble** (oats, chia, beans, lentils, apples, blueberries) — lowers
    blood glucose and cholesterol.
  - **Insoluble** (whole wheat/bran, quinoa, brown rice, legumes, leafy greens,
    nuts, seeds, fruit skins) — digestive regularity.
- **Fermentable fibre feeds gut bacteria** — the mechanistic link to microbiome
  health. Beta-glucans (oats), resistant starch, etc.
- Benefits: slows digestion, blunts post-meal blood-sugar spikes, feeds healthy
  gut colonies, supports satiety and weight control, lowers cardiovascular risk.

**Coaching hook:** fibre is almost always the fastest quality win — it rides on
foods Adam already likes (oats, bean mix, berries) and just needs a bit more
volume/variety.

## 3. Gut Microbiome & Plant Diversity (Tim Spector / ZOE)

Prof. Tim Spector (King's College London; ZOE; author of *Spoon-Fed*, *Food for
Life*):
- **"Aim for ~30 different plants a week"** — plant *diversity* (not just
  quantity) is the strongest dietary predictor of a healthy, diverse gut
  microbiome (American Gut Project data). Each distinct plant feeds different
  microbes.
- **What counts as a plant:** vegetables, fruits, legumes, whole grains, nuts,
  seeds, **herbs and spices** (small amounts count). Different colours/varieties
  count separately.
- A diverse microbiome is linked to better immune function, metabolic health,
  reduced inflammation, and mental wellbeing (gut-brain axis).
- Spector's broader message: **eat a wide range of real foods; minimise
  ultra-processed foods; be sceptical of "low-fat"/reductionist claims.**

**Coaching hook:** Adam's efficient ~15-food rotation is the enemy here.
Diversity, not restriction, is the lever — add new plants, don't remove things.

## 4. Ultra-Processed Foods (UPFs) & the NOVA Classification

Harvard Nutrition Source (Processed Foods) + NOVA (Monteiro et al.; WHO/FAO/
PAHO-recognised):
- **NOVA's four groups:**
  1. **Unprocessed / minimally processed** — fresh/frozen veg & fruit, whole
     grains, nuts, meats, eggs, plain yogurt, milk, dried pasta, coffee/tea.
  2. **Processed culinary ingredients** — oils, butter, salt, sugar.
  3. **Processed foods** — canned veg/fish, cheese, fresh bread (few
     ingredients, still recognisable).
  4. **Ultra-processed (UPF)** — industrial formulations with additives,
     emulsifiers, sweeteners; e.g. many packaged snacks, sauces, sugary drinks,
     processed meats, some breads/tortillas.
- **Not all processing is bad** (freezing, canning, fermentation are fine/
  useful). The concern is **UPFs specifically**.
- Growing evidence links **high UPF intake to worse cardiometabolic outcomes,
  weight gain and chronic-disease risk** — and some of it appears **independent
  of macros** (UPFs may drive overconsumption and displace whole foods).

**Coaching hook:** the goal is *pattern* — UPFs as occasional, not as the
structural anchor of meals. NOVA is a lens, not a morality test.

## 5. The Mediterranean Diet & Longevity (Walter Willett / PREDIMED)

- **PREDIMED** (Estruch et al., *NEJM* 2013; re-analysed 2018): a Mediterranean
  diet supplemented with extra-virgin olive oil or nuts produced **~30% relative
  reduction in major cardiovascular events** vs a low-fat control — one of the
  strongest RCT signals for a dietary *pattern* and health.
- **Pattern components:** abundant vegetables, fruit, legumes, whole grains,
  nuts; **olive oil as the primary fat**; **fish** regularly; moderate poultry/
  dairy; **little red/processed meat**; minimal added sugar.
- Dr. Walter Willett (Harvard) — decades of epidemiology support a plant-forward,
  whole-food, healthy-fat pattern for longevity and reduced chronic disease.
- **Diet as a pattern beats diet as individual nutrients** — the Med pattern is
  the most robustly evidenced framework for eating for long-term health.

**Coaching hook:** Adam is already close (olive oil, beans, plenty of plants) —
nudging toward the Med pattern is realistic, not a diet overhaul. (He doesn't
eat fish, so lean on legumes/poultry for protein and walnuts + a supplement for
omega-3 rather than the fish leg of the pattern.)

## 6. Micronutrients, Omega-3 & "Eat the Rainbow"

- **Colour variety ≈ micronutrient coverage:** different plant pigments carry
  different phytonutrients/antioxidants (e.g. leafy greens → folate/vitamin K;
  orange veg → beta-carotene; berries → polyphenols). No single food covers the
  spectrum — variety is the mechanism.
- **Omega-3 (EPA/DHA) — Adam's constraint:** Adam dislikes fish; **canned tuna
  is the only fish he'll eat** and it carries negligible EPA/DHA, so oily fish
  (salmon/mackerel/sardines) is **not an option** — do not recommend it. Route
  omega-3 two ways: (1) **plant ALA** daily from walnuts, chia, ground flax/
  linseed and rapeseed oil (ALA converts only partially to EPA/DHA, but still
  beneficial); (2) an **omega-3 supplement** — algae oil delivers vegan EPA/DHA,
  or standard fish/cod-liver capsules if acceptable. Defer dose to Layne. Canned
  tuna still counts as a fine **lean protein**, just not an omega-3 source.
- **Vitamin D:** commonly low, especially with limited sun/indoor lifestyles —
  worth flagging as a *health* (not performance) consideration; supplement
  decision → Layne.
- **Food-first principle:** whole foods deliver micronutrients in a matrix
  (fibre, co-factors) that isolated supplements don't replicate. Supplement only
  to fill a genuine, identified gap.

## 7. Non-Dogmatic Framing (shared with Layne, applied to quality)

- **Judge the pattern over weeks/months, never a single meal.** A hotdog night
  or maple syrup on pancakes is irrelevant to health if the *pattern* is good.
- **Add, don't restrict.** The evidence-based, sustainable lever is *adding*
  quality foods (more plants, more fibre, more variety), not banning "bad" ones.
  Restriction breeds rebound; addition crowds out the less-good foods naturally.
- **No moralising.** "Clean eating" is not a scientific concept. Spector cares
  about diversity, fibre, UPF load and pattern — not food purity.
- **Complement, don't contradict, Layne.** When health-quality and training-
  fuelling advice tension (e.g. veg volume vs calorie-dense training carbs),
  surface the trade-off; Twight synthesises.
